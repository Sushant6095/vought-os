# Three-Act Narrative

> The product story. Appears in marketing, onboarding, the product itself, and the demo video.

## Act 1 · The problem

Every important conversation in your life happens once.

You don't get to rehearse the job interview. You don't get to redo the first date. You don't get to take back what you said to your dying grandfather, or the call where your biggest customer told you they were leaving.

These conversations carry enormous weight. We walk into all of them with the same brain we use to order pizza.

So people freeze. They forget the question they wanted to ask. They miss the moment to say the right thing. They walk out of the meeting and immediately think, *I should have said X.*

## Act 2 · The reveal

The AI is already in the room with you.

Vought listens to your live conversation. It understands what's happening. And in under a second — fast enough that no one across from you notices — it whispers the next line into your earbud. In your own cloned voice. So you hear yourself, then you say it.

## Act 3 · The transformation

The conversation goes differently.

Deals close. Interviews land. Hard conversations resolve.

The user does not become someone else. They become a faster, calmer, more articulate version of themselves.

## Where the narrative appears

- **Landing hero** — problem condensed into one headline ("The voice that closes the deal"), reveal in the dashboard preview, transformation in the metrics row.
- **Onboarding** — Step 1 is the problem ("Pick the conversation that's coming"), Step 2 the reveal (voice clone), Step 3 the transformation (start your first call).
- **Demo video** — Act 1 (12s, silent failure), Act 2 (45s, AI emerges), Act 3 (30s, deal closed).
- **Investor pitch** — exactly the same three acts.

## When to invoke this narrative

Every external communication should land at one of the three acts. If a piece of copy doesn't, it's tangential and should probably be cut.

## Hard rules

- Never start with the technology. Always start with the human moment.
- Never explain how the AI works in Act 1 or Act 2. Save that for the architecture diagram.
- The transformation is always specific (a named outcome), never abstract.
