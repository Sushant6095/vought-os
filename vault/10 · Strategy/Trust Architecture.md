# Trust Architecture

> Fifteen specific places trust is built. Every one must be present.

## On the marketing site

1. Product name in metadata title and favicon — consistent everywhere.
2. Customer logos high on landing, in their typefaces, neutral grey.
3. Real metrics — latency in milliseconds, customer ARR influenced, suggestion acceptance %.
4. A named case study with avatar, title, and company. *Not* "a sales team in San Francisco."
5. A linked security page from footer and nav.
6. SOC 2 / HIPAA / GDPR badges with real status (in-progress is fine; vague is not).
7. A specific architecture diagram. Engineers will pause on it.
8. A public changelog. Frequent updates signal a real engineering org.
9. A bug bounty / security email on the Security page.

## In the product

10. "No audio recorded" disclosure visible on every live screen.
11. Explicit voice clone consent flow — never buried.
12. Source attribution on every AI suggestion (which playbook, which version).
13. Confidence indicator on every suggestion when uncertainty matters.

## In the company

14. Founder names on About page with real Twitter/LinkedIn.
15. A real San Francisco office address in the footer.
16. A working contact email with a 24-hour response promise.

## Trust is built by what we refuse

- We refuse to use "AI-powered" as an adjective.
- We refuse vague case studies ("a Fortune 500").
- We refuse to manufacture urgency.
- We refuse generic stock photography.
- We refuse to hide pricing.
- We refuse to talk down to the operator.

## Specificity is the brand's signature

Every trust signal is more specific than its category demands.
- Other companies say "low latency." We say *412ms*.
- Other companies say "customers love it." We say *TripleByte's acceptance rate tripled*.
- Other companies say "compliant." We say *SOC 2 Type II, audited annually*.
